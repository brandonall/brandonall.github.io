Gospel: Matthew 2:13-18

When the magi had departed, behold, the angel of the Lord appeared to Joseph in a dream and said, “Get up, take the child and his mother, flee to Egypt, and stay there until I tell you. Herod is going to search for the child to destroy him.” Joseph rose and took the child and his mother by night and departed for Egypt. He stayed there until the death of Herod, that what the Lord had said through the prophet might be fulfilled, Out of Egypt I called my son. When Herod realized that he had been deceived by the magi, he became furious. He ordered the massacre of all the boys in Bethlehem and its vicinity two years old and under, in accordance with the time he had ascertained from the magi. Then was fulfilled what had been said through Jeremiah the prophet: A voice was heard in Ramah, sobbing and loud lamentation: Rachel weeping for her children, and she would not be consoled, since they were no more.

Daily Reflection Questions

  • The Holy Innocents suffered for Christ. How does their sacrifice remind me of the cost of following Jesus?
  • Herod’s fear led to violence. What fears in my life might cause me to harm others, and how can I surrender them to God?
  • Rachel weeping for her children. How can I bring comfort to those who mourn loss or injustice today?
  • God protected the Holy Family. Where do I need God’s protection and guidance in my own family or relationships?
  • The innocents are honored as martyrs. How can I honor the vulnerable and voiceless in my community?
  • The feast reminds us of evil in the world. How does Christ’s birth give me hope in the face of suffering?

Commentary

The Feast of the Holy Innocents reminds us that the joy of Christ’s birth is not without suffering. Herod’s fear led to violence, but God protected the Holy Family. The readings call us to trust in God’s providence and to honor the vulnerable, even in a world marked by evil. This feast falls within the Christmas octave, showing that the light of Christ shines brightest in darkness.

Liturgical Context

This feast occurs during the Christmas octave, a time of extended celebration of the Nativity. It reminds us that the joy of Christ’s birth is not without cost, as evil opposes the light from the very beginning.

Fun Facts & Connections

  • The Holy Innocents are considered the first martyrs of the Church, even before Christ’s death on the cross.
  • The slaughter of the innocents fulfills the prophecy of Jeremiah about Rachel weeping for her children.
  • The feast is a stark reminder that the world into which Jesus was born was hostile to Him from the start.
  • December 28 is also known as “Childermas” in some traditions, a day historically associated with children.
